## Science-Backed Back Stabilization Exercises (The McGill Big 3)
Based on research, the most effective and evidence-based exercises for back stabilization are:

1. **McGill Curl-up**
   - **Target**: Rectus abdominis without spinal flexion.
   - **Execution**: Lie on back, one leg straight, one knee bent. Hands under lower back to maintain natural curve. Lift head and shoulders slightly off the floor. Hold for 10 seconds.
   - **Benefit**: Builds endurance in the abdominal wall while protecting the spine.

2. **Side Plank (Side Bridge)**
   - **Target**: Obliques and quadratus lumborum.
   - **Execution**: Lie on side, propped on elbow. Lift hips to form a straight line from head to feet. Hold for 10 seconds.
   - **Benefit**: Essential for lateral stability and reducing back pain.

3. **Bird-Dog (Quadruped)**
   - **Target**: Back extensors (erector spinae) and glutes.
   - **Execution**: On all fours, extend opposite arm and leg simultaneously. Keep back flat and hips level. Hold for 10 seconds.
   - **Benefit**: Improves coordination and stability of the posterior chain.

4. **Dead Bug** (Additional Research-Backed)
   - **Target**: Deep core (transverse abdominis).
   - **Execution**: Lie on back, arms up, knees at 90 degrees. Lower opposite arm and leg slowly while keeping lower back pressed to the floor.

5. **Pallof Press** (Anti-Rotation)
   - **Target**: Core stability against rotational forces.
   - **Execution**: Stand sideways to a resistance band/cable. Hold handle at chest, push out and hold against the pull.

- Implement `Hero` transitions for seamless navigation between list and detail views.
